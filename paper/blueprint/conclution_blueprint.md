# Paragraph 1 — Restate the Scientific Problem

### Objective

Reintroduce the scientific question.

### Key Message

Gravity-model calibration traditionally depends on observed origin–destination interactions.

The study investigates whether aggregate travel-distance observations alone are sufficient for parameter identification.

### Constraint

Do not mention PIGF or implementation details.

---

# Paragraph 2 — Scientific Discovery

### Objective

State the principal finding.

### Key Message

Aggregate travel-distance distributions preserve sufficient statistical information to identify latent city-specific distance-deterrence parameters.

The identified parameters are statistically consistent with those obtained from complete OD observations and preserve the predictive consequences of gravity-model calibration.

This is the primary scientific contribution of the paper.

---

# Paragraph 3 — Methodological Contribution

### Objective

Present the framework as the consequence of the discovery.

### Key Message

Building on this discovery, the proposed probabilistic calibration framework identifies latent distance-deterrence parameters directly from aggregate travel-distance observations and integrates them with independently estimated production and attraction components for survey-free gravity calibration.

OD prediction serves as the downstream validation of successful parameter identification.

---

# Paragraph 4 — Conceptual Contribution

### Objective

Describe the broader conceptual implication.

### Key Message

The study suggests a shift in how survey-free spatial interaction modeling is formulated.

Rather than treating aggregate mobility products as incomplete substitutes for OD matrices, they should be regarded as statistical observation layers whose information content determines whether latent spatial interaction parameters can be identified.

This reframes survey-free mobility modeling as a parameter-identification problem under limited observations.

---

# Paragraph 5 — Future Vision

### Objective

End with a broader scientific perspective.

### Key Message

Although the present study is limited to gravity-model calibration and aggregate travel-distance distributions, the results suggest a broader research direction.

Future survey-free spatial interaction modeling may depend less on reconstructing complete origin–destination matrices and more on understanding which aggregate observations preserve sufficient statistical information for identifying latent mechanisms governing human mobility.

Extending this observation-centered perspective to richer aggregate representations and more general spatial interaction models provides a promising direction for future research.
